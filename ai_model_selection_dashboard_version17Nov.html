01

Define Your Organization’s AI Needs

Consider eight areas that shape how your organization may select and operate generative AI
⚙1. Who would operate the AI system?

Think about who would be responsible for setting up, monitoring, updating, and maintaining the AI system after it is introduced.

▦2. Do you already have GPU infrastructure available for AI workloads?

Running AI models internally may require specialized computing resources such as GPUs. Does your organization already have these resources available for AI use?

◈3. Can the intended data be sent to an external AI provider?

Consider the data that users or applications may send to the AI system. Are there privacy, security, policy, or other restrictions on sending this data outside your organization?

☁4. Are you already committed to a cloud/provider ecosystem?

For example, your organization may already rely heavily on AWS, Azure, or Google for cloud services, identity, data, or applications.

$5. Which costs have you evaluated?

AI costs can extend beyond the advertised subscription or API price. Select the costs your organization has already considered.

⇄6. Would changing approach require rebuilding integrations or workflows?

Think about systems connected to your current AI service, such as login, applications, data pipelines, or work processes. Would moving to another approach require changes to them?

⌂7. Do you need the model to run inside infrastructure you control?

Some organizations may require the AI model or its data processing to remain within infrastructure they directly control rather than relying entirely on an external provider.

08

Sustainability & Resource Use

Consider whether environmental resource use and different model choices should be part of your organization’s evaluation.

♧8A. Do you want energy and water use considered in model selection?

AI models can differ in the computing resources they use. Select Yes if environmental resource use should be considered when examining model choices.

↔8B. Could your organization consider using different AI models for different types of tasks?

Different AI tasks may not require the same level of model capability. Using the same large model for every task may use more computing resources than necessary.
